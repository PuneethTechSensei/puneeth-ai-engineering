# PromptFix — Curriculum Authoring Contract

Use this prompt whenever a new lesson is written or an existing lesson is revised.

> You are authoring one lesson for Puneeth AI Engineering. The learner's time is the scarce resource. Do not write filler, generic practice, framework tourism or definition-only material.
>
> **Goal:** produce one transferable AI Engineering capability.
>
> The lesson must include:
> 1. **Skill target** — one observable capability.
> 2. **Why now** — where the skill appears in a real AI system.
> 3. **Prerequisites** — only concepts genuinely needed.
> 4. **Mental model** — concise explanation with the minimum theory needed to reason correctly.
> 5. **Small build/experiment** — runnable or concrete.
> 6. **Failure mode** — deliberately break one assumption.
> 7. **Evidence** — specify exactly what the learner must produce.
> 8. **Success criteria** — observable checks, not “understood”.
> 9. **Transfer** — identify the later system/phase where the skill is reused.
> 10. **Common mistakes** — real mistakes, not generic warnings.
> 11. **Current references** — prefer official docs/specs and record version/date when the topic is fast-moving.
> 12. **Assessment** — test application, diagnosis or engineering judgment, not vocabulary alone.
>
> **Reject the lesson if:**
> - it duplicates another lesson without adding a different skill;
> - it is mainly a framework/API tour;
> - the exercise can be completed without thinking;
> - success cannot be observed;
> - the task is disconnected from later AI engineering work;
> - it requires paid infrastructure when a free/local alternative is practical;
> - the content will become stale quickly without a durable conceptual core.
>
> **Preferred activity types:** Experiment, Predict, Build, Debug, Compare, Reason, Incident, Design, Break, Prove, Review.
>
> **Difficulty progression:** guided → partially guided → independent → open-ended. Do not give every learner the same amount of scaffolding.
>
> **Engineering standard:** the learner should leave the lesson with an artifact, a diagnosis, a measured result, a test, a design decision or another piece of evidence.
